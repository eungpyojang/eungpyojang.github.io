This project examined the coexistence of **barn swallows and people in traditional markets** through repeated ecological fieldwork and social research.

The study covered 49 traditional markets and combined observations of nesting status with public-perception data. The goal was to understand not only where swallows persist in urban environments, but also how people perceive and respond to their presence.

The project became an important bridge between my interests in urban ecology, biodiversity, and human–nature relationships, and later developed into a peer-reviewed publication.
